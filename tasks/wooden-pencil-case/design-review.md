# Wooden pencil case design review

**Instruction.** Open the wooden pencil case. Turn and hold the orange release knob with one arm while the other lifts out the pen. Hold the pen clear of the case.

**Starting state and impediment.** The wooden lid starts closed. The pen sits lengthwise in two fitted saddles with finger relief in the middle. A separate orange knob and linked steel keeper share a vertical shaft. The spring biases two tabs over the barrel. Turning the knob moves both tabs sideways and gives the lifting gripper a clear path. Vertical pulling loads the tabs across the turn axis. Releasing the knob drives the tabs back toward the locked position. Both arms start at the YAM ready pose, with a fixed layout at seed 0.

**Real precedent and overlap.** Wooden pen boxes with brass hinges and fitted inserts are sold as stationery packaging. Spring-return rotary latches are commercial mechanisms. **Observable failures and alternatives.** A pull against the closed keeper should stall, and an early knob release should return the tabs before the pen lifts clear. Tilt, axial sliding and holding the knob open with the extracting arm are candidate shortcuts. The tabs straddle the available central grasp, the saddle walls limit sideways motion, and a valid lift requires opposing fingertip forces on the knob and pen from distinct arms as the pen clears the keeper. Either arm can take either role. The code measures actual contacts and poses.

**Rejection criteria.** Reject a layout that lets the pen lift through the locked keeper, bind against the open lid or case walls, stay unlocked after release, or receive full credit from a single arm or an uncontrolled drop. Check a full scripted extraction, a locked pull and a turn-then-release pull from separate native resets. Reject the overhead recording if it cannot show the knob turn and the removed pen.
